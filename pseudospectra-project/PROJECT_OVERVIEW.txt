================================================================================
MATHEMATICAL CONTENT
================================================================================

KEY CONCEPT:
  A matrix can be SPECTRALLY STABLE (all eigenvalues have negative real part)
  yet exhibit DRAMATIC TRANSIENT GROWTH in ‖exp(tA)‖ if it is non-normal.
  
  Example: 2×2 matrix with eigenvalues at -1 and -2 can have peak
  ‖exp(tA)‖ exceeding 100 before decaying.

PSEUDOSPECTRA:
  The ε-pseudospectrum is: Λ_ε(A) = {z ∈ ℂ : σ_min(A - zI) ≤ ε}
  
  - For normal matrices: just ε-disks around eigenvalues
  - For non-normal matrices: can bulge FAR from eigenvalues
  - This bulging region reveals hidden instability

KREISS MATRIX THEOREM:
  K(A) = sup_{Re(z)>0} Re(z) / σ_min(A - zI)
  
  Bounds:  K(A) ≤ sup_t ‖exp(tA)‖ ≤ e·n·K(A)
  
  - Lower bound is tight (hard to improve)
  - Upper bound is typically loose for structured matrices
  - Your theorem improves the e·n factor!

RESEARCH TARGET:
  For your chosen matrix family (shifted superdiagonal or advection-diffusion),
  prove a bound of the form:
  
    sup_t ‖exp(tA)‖ ≤ f(n, c, Pe, ...)
  
  where f is sharper than e·n·K(A). Example targets:
  - O(exp(αc)) instead of O(n·exp(...))
  - O(√n) instead of O(n)
  - O(log n) instead of O(n)

  2. Trefethen et al. (1993) — The "eigenvalues lie" paper
